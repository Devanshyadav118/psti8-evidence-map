C. KNOWLEDGE SUMMARY

Established
- ARRIVE 2.0 reporting guidelines for in vivo animal research: Updated in 2020 by an international working group following a Delphi exercise with 73 panelists from 19 countries, ARRIVE 2.0 reorganized 21 reporting items into two prioritized tiers: the "ARRIVE Essential 10" (study design, sample size and a priori power calculation, inclusion/exclusion criteria, randomization, blinding, outcome measures, statistical methods and assumptions, experimental animals including species, strain, sex, age, and weight, experimental procedures, and results with summary statistics and precision) and the "Recommended Set" (11 items providing context such as abstract, background, objectives, ethical statement, housing/husbandry, animal care/monitoring, interpretation, generalizability, protocol registration, data access, and declaration of interests) (`perciedusert2020_plosbiol`, `perciedusert2020_ee_plosbiol`, `soliman2023_bmjos`). ARRIVE 2.0 explicitly distinguishes between the biological replicate/experimental unit (the independent entity randomized to treatment) and repeated or technical measurements on a single animal to prevent pseudoreplication (`perciedusert2020_ee_plosbiol`).
- NIH Sex as a Biological Variable (SABV) policy and longitudinal audits of sex inclusion in general and metabolic animal research: Implemented in January 2016 (`arnegard2020_jwh`), the NIH SABV policy requires investigators to factor sex into experimental design, analysis, and reporting in vertebrate animal and human studies (`beery2018_currtop`, `arnegard2020_jwh`, `woitowich2020_elife`, `rich2024_diabetes`). Before SABV, a 2009 survey across 10 biological fields found male bias in 8 disciplines (most severely in neuroscience at 5.5:1 males to females, pharmacology at 5.0:1, and physiology at 3.7:1), with over 60% of studies that used both sexes failing to analyze results by sex (`beery2011_neubiorev`), while an audit of 1,363 surgical animal/cell papers (2011–2012) found 22% omitted sex and 80% of sex-reporting papers used only males (`yoon2014_surgery`). In a 10-year follow-up of 720 articles published in 2019 across 34 journals in 9 disciplines, sex-inclusive studies increased from 28% (2009) to 49% (2019), but the proportion of sex-inclusive studies that actually analyzed data by sex declined from 50% to 42% (and failed to improve in 8 of 9 disciplines) (`woitowich2020_elife`). Specifically in diabetes and metabolic research, a 25-year audit of 850 nonhuman animal studies published in *Diabetes* (1999, 2004, 2009, 2014, 2019, and 2023) found that while reporting of animal sex improved from <70% prior to 2019 to 93% in 2023, 58% of sex-reporting studies in 2023 still used a single sex (85% of which used only males), and fewer than 25% of studies that included both sexes analyzed their data by sex (`rich2024_diabetes`).
- Statistical testing of sex-by-treatment interactions and subgroup testing fallacies: Multiple independent methodological and empirical studies establish that claiming a treatment acts differently in males and females requires testing the formal statistical interaction term ($\text{Sex} \times \text{Treatment}$) in a factorial model (such as two-way ANOVA or a generalized linear mixed model), rather than testing males and females separately and comparing $p$-values (`garciasifuentes2021_elife`, `richedwards2023_bsd`, `phillips2023_plosbiol`). Testing the sexes separately and concluding a sex difference exists when one sex yields $p < 0.05$ and the other yields $p > 0.05$ is a pervasive statistical fallacy (inflating false-positive sex difference claims up to 50% when both subgroups have comparable power); in an audit of 147 sex-inclusive articles published in 2019, more than 70% of studies claiming a sex-specific treatment effect failed to statistically compare the sexes via an interaction test (`garciasifuentes2021_elife`, `richedwards2023_bsd`).
- Estrous cycle variability in female versus male rodents: Two large independent meta-analyses refuting the historical rationale for excluding female rodents—one analyzing 293 articles comprising 9,932 trait measurements in mice (`prendergast2014_neubiorev`) and a 2023 meta-analysis of 311 rodent exploratory behavior articles (`levy2023_neubiorev`)—demonstrated that unstaged female rodents tested across random stages of the estrous cycle are no more intrinsically variable than males across physiological, metabolic, morphological, molecular, and behavioral traits (and in several traits males exhibit greater variability due to group-housing social hierarchy) (`prendergast2014_neubiorev`, `beery2018_currtop`, `levy2023_neubiorev`). Consequently, routine estrous-cycle staging or doubling sample sizes to control for estrous stage is unnecessary unless estrous-stage modulation itself is the primary research question (`prendergast2014_neubiorev`, `beery2018_currtop`, `levy2023_neubiorev`).
- SYRCLE Risk of Bias tool and systematic review methodology for animal studies: Developed in 2014 by `Hooijmans` et al. (`hooijmans2014_bmcmrm`) by adapting the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool for animal intervention experiments, the SYRCLE RoB tool contains 10 domains across 6 bias categories: sequence generation (Item 1), baseline characteristics (Item 2), and allocation concealment (Item 3) for selection bias; random housing (Item 4) and caregiver/investigator blinding (Item 5) for performance bias; random outcome assessment (Item 6) and outcome assessor blinding (Item 7) for detection bias; incomplete outcome data (Item 8) for attrition bias; selective outcome reporting (Item 9) for reporting bias; and other sources of bias (Item 10, such as unit-of-analysis errors or inappropriate crossover design) (`hooijmans2014_bmcmrm`, `devries2014_ilarj`, `soliman2023_bmjos`). A 2023 systematic review of 42 preclinical systematic review guidance documents (`soliman2023_bmjos`, selected from 12,188 screened records) synthesized 85 best-practice steps spanning multidisciplinary team formation, PICO question formulation, protocol registration, comprehensive multi-database searching without restricting to English, dual independent screening and data extraction, SYRCLE RoB assessment, and random-effects meta-analysis (`devries2014_ilarj`, `soliman2023_bmjos`).
- PRISMA 2020, PRISMA-S, and prospective protocol registration (PROSPERO, PreclinicalTrials.eu, OSF): The PRISMA 2020 statement (`page2021_bmj`, 27 checklist items plus an updated 12-item abstract checklist and flow diagrams) and its PRISMA-S search extension (`rethlefsen2021_systrev`, 16 items requiring exact full search strategies for every database, register, and browse interface) govern transparent evidence synthesis reporting (`page2021_bmj`, `rethlefsen2021_systrev`, `soliman2023_bmjos`). For prospective protocol registration, systematic reviews of animal studies relevant to human health can be registered in PROSPERO (where an international survey showed 99% support for registering preclinical reviews and 3,131 animal systematic review protocols were registered between 2018 and 2023) or on the Open Science Framework (OSF) (`booth2020_systrev`, `soliman2023_bmjos`, `vandernaald2024_jce`). For primary animal experiments, `PreclinicalTrials.eu` (launched in 2018) provides a dedicated prospective protocol registry; a 5-year inventory of 141 registered protocols from 24 countries showed that registered animal protocols reported blinding in 72%, randomization in 70%, and sample size calculations in 61%—substantially higher than the general published animal literature (`vandervaart2020_jaha`, `vandernaald2024_jce`).
- Validation of Large Language Models (LLMs) for systematic review screening and data extraction (2024–2025): Across multiple independent benchmarking studies and a 2025 living systematic review of 175 studies (`gartlehner2024_aim`, `guo2024_jce`, `konet2024_systrev`, `yun2024_jamia`, `delgadochaves2025_bmcmrm`, `lieberum2025_medrxiv`), LLMs are most widely applied to title/abstract screening (40.0% of studies in `lieberum2025_medrxiv`) and data extraction (30.9% of studies) (`lieberum2025_medrxiv`). In title/abstract screening, `Guo` et al. (`guo2024_jce`) evaluated GPT-4 across 24,682 records from 6 systematic reviews, finding 91% overall accuracy, 91% specificity for excluding non-eligible studies, and 76% sensitivity for included studies, while `Delgado-Chaves` et al. (`delgadochaves2025_bmcmrm`) benchmarked 18 open- and closed-source LLMs across 3 biomedical systematic reviews (1,766 abstracts; 78 full texts), showing workload reductions up to 96.3% and finding that a majority-vote ensemble of 3 open-source LLMs (`Mistral-7B`, `Mixtral-8x7B`, `BioMistral`) matched or exceeded commercial GPT-4 performance. For categorical/study-characteristic data extraction, `Gartlehner` et al. (`gartlehner2024_aim`) found Claude 2 achieved 96.3% accuracy across 1,410 data elements in 10 RCTs (only 52 elements incorrect; sensitivities 87.5%–100%), and `Konet` et al. (`konet2024_systrev`) found GPT-4 and Claude 2 achieved >80% overall accuracy (and 95% accuracy on sample size) across 109 studies (`gartlehner2024_aim`, `konet2024_systrev`).

Contested
- Sample size requirements for including both sexes vs. detecting a $\text{Sex} \times \text{Treatment}$ interaction (`phillips2023_plosbiol` vs. `richedwards2023_bsd`): A widespread misconception holds that including both males and females requires doubling the total sample size (`beery2018_currtop`, `phillips2023_plosbiol`). Using extensive statistical power simulations across 9 biological scenarios (authored by the Karp laboratory; single-lab simulation study building on factorial design theory), `Phillips` et al. (`phillips2023_plosbiol`) demonstrated that splitting an existing total sample size $N$ equally between males and females ($N/2$ per sex) in a two-way ANOVA factorial design results in **no loss of statistical power** to detect the main treatment effect when treatment effects are consistent in direction across sexes (even when baseline sex differences are large, because two-way ANOVA partitions out baseline sex variance), and actually **increases power** when a qualitative sex interaction exists compared with pooling or ignoring sex. However, both `phillips2023_plosbiol` and `richedwards2023_bsd` emphasize that powered detection of a moderate quantitative $\text{Sex} \times \text{Treatment}$ interaction (e.g., an attenuation where treatment works twice as strongly in one sex as the other) requires a substantially larger total sample size (conventionally up to 4-fold more animals to achieve 80% interaction power than to detect a main effect of the same magnitude) (`richedwards2023_bsd`, `phillips2023_plosbiol`).
- Zero-shot LLM accuracy on complex numeric/continuous outcome extraction vs. study characteristics (`gartlehner2024_aim` and `konet2024_systrev` vs. `yun2024_jamia`): Whereas `gartlehner2024_aim` and `konet2024_systrev` reported high accuracy (80%–96.3%) for Claude 2 and GPT-4 when extracting study design characteristics, sample sizes, and structured PICO fields from RCTs, `Yun` et al. (`yun2024_jamia`) evaluated 7 LLMs across 328 PubMed Central systematic reviews (628,434 tokens) for automated extraction of numerical meta-analytic outcomes and found only moderate exact-match agreement with human extractors—with the best model (GPT-4) achieving 51.31% exact match on binary outcome tables (`Llama-2-70B` 41.32%) and only 21.17% exact match on continuous outcome tables (`Konet` et al. similarly noted lower accuracy on numeric outcomes in `konet2024_systrev`)—establishing that LLM extraction of numerical effect estimates cannot yet replace human verification (`konet2024_systrev`, `yun2024_jamia`, `lieberum2025_medrxiv`).

Unknown
- Empirical compliance with ARRIVE 2.0 and SYRCLE items in preclinical peptide therapeutics for metabolic disease: While field-wide audits exist for general diabetes (`rich2024_diabetes`), surgery (`yoon2014_surgery`), and 9 broad biological disciplines (`woitowich2020_elife`), no published multi-journal systematic audit has quantified how often preclinical studies of chromogranin A/pancreastatin-derived peptides report a priori power calculations, allocation concealment, random housing, or blinded outcome assessment (`hooijmans2014_bmcmrm`, `perciedusert2020_plosbiol`, `rich2024_diabetes`).
- Validated accuracy of LLMs specifically for SYRCLE risk-of-bias assessment in animal studies: In the 2025 living systematic review of 175 LLM automation studies (`lieberum2025_medrxiv`), risk-of-bias evaluations (18.9% of included studies) almost exclusively tested Cochrane RoB 2 or ROBINS-I on human clinical trials rather than the 10-item SYRCLE tool for animal experiments (`hooijmans2014_bmcmrm`, `lieberum2025_medrxiv`).

D. CONTRADICTIONS

- Improved sex reporting vs. stagnant/declining sex-based analysis (`woitowich2020_elife` and `rich2024_diabetes`): Both `woitowich2020_elife` (across 9 disciplines, 2009 vs. 2019) and `rich2024_diabetes` (across 25 years in *Diabetes*, 1999–2023) show a stark contradiction between policy compliance in *reporting/including* sex (rising to 49% both-sex inclusion in `woitowich2020_elife` and 93% sex specification in `rich2024_diabetes`) and compliance in *statistical analysis by sex*, which declined from 50% (2009) to 42% (2019) in `woitowich2020_elife` and remained below 25% of sex-inclusive studies in `rich2024_diabetes`.
- High LLM accuracy on study metadata vs. low exact-match accuracy on continuous numerical outcome tables (`gartlehner2024_aim` and `konet2024_systrev` vs. `yun2024_jamia`): `gartlehner2024_aim` reported 96.3% accuracy for Claude 2 across 1,410 structured RCT elements and `konet2024_systrev` reported 95% accuracy on sample size extraction, whereas `yun2024_jamia` demonstrated across 328 systematic reviews that even GPT-4 achieved only 51.31% exact match on binary numerical outcomes and 21.17% exact match on continuous numerical outcomes when extracting raw study numbers for meta-analysis (`gartlehner2024_aim`, `konet2024_systrev`, `yun2024_jamia`).

E. OPEN QUESTIONS

- [Inference] In the PSTi8 preclinical literature—where only 2 of 11 catalogued studies (`valicherla2022_pk` and `tshikudi2024_colitis`) included both male and female animals and 0 metabolic disease studies used a both-sex factorial design—could splitting a standard $n = 12\text{--}16$ treatment cohort into $n = 6\text{--}8$ males and $n = 6\text{--}8$ females per group and analyzing via two-way ANOVA (`Treatment + Sex + Treatment:Sex`, as validated in `phillips2023_plosbiol`) detect main metabolic effects with equivalent power while screening for large qualitative sex interactions?
