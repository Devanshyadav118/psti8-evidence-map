C. KNOWLEDGE SUMMARY

Established
- Endoplasmic reticulum stress and GRP78/UPR in obesity and insulin resistance: Obesity and nutrient overload induce ER stress in liver and adipose tissue, where unfolded proteins sequester the luminal HSP70 chaperone GRP78 (BiP/HSPA5, 654-aa precursor with an 18-aa N-terminal signal peptide, 635-aa mature protein, and C-terminal KDEL motif), releasing the transmembrane sensors PERK, IRE1$\alpha$, and ATF6 (`ozcan2004_science`, `samanta2021_canres`, `li2026_theranostics`). Hyperactivation of the IRE1$\alpha$–JNK branch promotes inhibitory serine phosphorylation of IRS-1 and impairs insulin receptor signaling (`ozcan2004_science`). Conversely, restoring hepatic ER folding capacity via adenoviral GRP78 overexpression in *ob/ob* mice suppresses ER stress markers, blocks both insulin- and ER stress-driven SREBP-1c cleavage, restores IRS-2 expression and tyrosine phosphorylation, and reduces hepatic steatosis (`kammoun2009_jci`).
- Cell-surface and circulating GRP78 as a signaling receptor and metabolic driver: Under ER stress or pathological conditions, a subfraction of GRP78 escapes KDEL-mediated ER retention (including via an IRE1$\alpha$–SRC–ASAP1 pathway or exosomal secretion regulated by HDAC6-mediated Lys633 deacetylation) and localizes to the plasma membrane (`csGRP78`/`sGRP78`) or circulation (`zhang2013_plosone`, `angelini2022_natcommun`, `lee2025_pnas`, `li2026_theranostics`). At the cell surface, `sGRP78` complexes with the p85 regulatory subunit of PI3K to increase $\text{PI(3,4,5)P}_3$ production and activate PI3K/AKT survival signaling (as well as inhibiting PP2A-mediated Akt dephosphorylation at Thr308/Ser473), an interaction disrupted by an N-terminal domain insertion mutation in GRP78 (`zhang2013_plosone`, `li2026_theranostics`). In metabolic disease, serum HSP70 and GRP78 are elevated in human NASH patients and decline after metabolic surgery; continuous infusion of recombinant GRP78 and HSP70 in chow-fed rats induces hepatic and whole-body insulin resistance, hyperglycemia, and NASH, whereas neutralizing monoclonal antibodies against GRP78/HSP70 reverse these phenotypes in high-fat-diet (HFD) rats (`angelini2022_natcommun`, `pan2022_ijerph`).
- GRP78 structure, post-translational modifications, and ATPase inhibitors: GRP78 comprises an N-terminal nucleotide-binding/ATPase domain (NBD, sharing >90% homology across human, mouse, and rat) connected via a 10-aa flexible linker to a C-terminal substrate-binding domain ($\text{SBD}\beta$ pocket and $\text{SBD}\alpha$ helical lid) (`li2026_theranostics`). Its activity is regulated by eight reversible post-translational modifications, including SBD phosphorylation (Thr453, Thr473, Thr500), ADP-ribosylation (Arg470, Arg492 by hARTC1, which reduces unfolded protein aggregation by 40%–65%), METTL21A-mediated Lys585 trimethylation, UHRF1/GP78 Lys48-linked ubiquitination (counteracted by deubiquitinase OTUD3), and competitive Cys420 S-palmitoylation (by ZDHHC9) versus $\text{H}_2\text{S}$-mediated S-sulfhydration (`li2026_theranostics`). Small molecules that inhibit GRP78 ATPase activity—either directly at the NBD or allosterically via the SBD—include `VER-155008` ($\text{IC}_{50} = 0.7 \pm 0.3\ \mu\text{M}$, non-selective for HSP70/GRP78), the hydroxyquinoline `YUM70` ($\text{IC}_{50} = 1.5 \pm 0.3\ \mu\text{M}$, which docks to SBD residues Ser452, Gln458, Thr462, and Lys464 without binding HSP70, PDI, or GSTO1), `HA15`, `sanguinarine` ($\text{IC}_{50} = 8.6\ \mu\text{M}$), and `EGCG` (`samanta2021_canres`, `pan2022_ijerph`, `li2026_theranostics`).
- Hepatic insulin receptor and IRS1/2–PI3K–AKT–FoxO1 signaling: Upon insulin binding to the $\alpha_2\beta_2$ insulin receptor (`InsR`), trans-autophosphorylation of $\beta$-subunit tyrosine residues (Tyr1146, Tyr1150, Tyr1151 in the catalytic loop; Tyr953/960 in the juxtamembrane domain; Tyr1316/1322 in the C-terminus) recruits IRS-1 and IRS-2, activating PI3K to generate $\text{PIP}_3$ and activate PDK1 (which phosphorylates Akt at Thr308) and mTORC2 (which phosphorylates Akt at Ser473) (`dong2006_jci`, `cruzvillarreal2026_kp`). In hepatocytes, IRS-1 and IRS-2 exhibit functional redundancy for acute Akt–FoxO1 activation (`dong2006_jci`) alongside temporal specialization during fasting/refeeding (`kubota2008_cmet`). Combined hepatocyte deletion of `Irs1` and `Irs2` (`LKO::Irs1-/-` or `DKO`, which reduces body size by 70% vs. WT) completely abrogates insulin-stimulated Akt–FoxO1 phosphorylation, unleashing nuclear FoxO1 to drive gluconeogenic transcription (`Pck1`, `G6pc`, `Ppargc1`, `Igfbp1`) and `Hmox1` (heme oxygenase-1), which disrupts mitochondrial respiratory complexes III and IV, lowers the $\text{NAD}^+/\text{NADH}$ ratio, and impairs ATP synthesis (`dong2006_jci`, `cheng2009_natmed`). Genetic deletion of hepatic `Foxo1` in `Irs1/Irs2` DKO mice normalizes `Hmox1`, reduces `Ppargc1a` acetylation, and restores mitochondrial oxidative metabolism and glucose homeostasis (`cheng2009_natmed`, `yang2026_cells`).
- GSK-3$\beta$ vs. GSK-3$\alpha$ and hepatic glycogen regulation: Although Akt phosphorylates and inactivates GSK-3$\alpha$ (at Ser21) and GSK-3$\beta$ (at Ser9) (`mcmanus2005_emboj`), multiple independent genetic models show that GSK-3$\beta$ is not the primary rate-limiting determinant of hepatic glycogen storage or whole-body glucose tolerance in liver: (1) in `LKO::Irs1-/-` mice lacking hepatic Akt–FoxO1 signaling, hepatic GSK-3$\beta$ Ser9 phosphorylation and glycogen stores remain nearly normal (`dong2006_jci`); (2) in homozygous `GSK3alpha(S21A)/GSK3beta(S9A)` knockin mice, insulin-stimulated muscle glycogen synthase activation requires GSK-3$\beta$ Ser9 phosphorylation, whereas glucose-stimulated hepatic glycogen synthase activation does not require Ser21/Ser9 phosphorylation (`mcmanus2005_emboj`); and (3) liver-specific `Gsk3b` knockout mice exhibit normal glucose/insulin tolerance and hepatic insulin signaling, whereas skeletal-muscle-specific `Gsk3b` knockout mice show enhanced insulin-stimulated glycogen synthase activation and improved glucose tolerance (`patel2008_mcb`).
- PKC isoforms ($\text{PKC}\lambda/\zeta$, $\text{PKC}\varepsilon$, $\text{PKC}\delta$) and SREBP-1c in selective hepatic insulin resistance: Downstream of PI3K, hepatic signaling bifurcates (`matsumoto2003_jci`, `taniguchi2006_cmet`, `cruzvillarreal2026_kp`). Atypical $\text{PKC}\lambda/\iota$ and $\text{PKC}\zeta$ (activated by $\text{PIP}_3$ binding to their pseudosubstrate domain and PDK1/mTORC2 phosphorylation, independent of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ or DAG) positively regulate GLUT4 translocation in muscle and adipocytes, but in liver $\text{PKC}\lambda$ drives insulin- and refeeding-induced `Srebp1c` and `Fas` expression (`matsumoto2003_jci`, `taniguchi2006_cmet`, `cruzvillarreal2026_kp`). Male liver-specific $\text{PKC}\lambda$ knockout (`L-lambdaKO`) mice exhibit an ~50% reduction in refeeding-induced hepatic `Srebp1c` mRNA (and ~35% and ~25% reductions in `Fas` and `Scd1`), lower hepatic triglyceride content, and increased systemic insulin sensitivity, without changes in `Pck1`/`G6pc` suppression or glycogen accumulation (`matsumoto2003_jci`). In parallel, Akt also stimulates hepatic SREBP-1c via both mTORC1-dependent signaling and mTORC1-independent suppression of the SREBP-1c inhibitor `Insig2a` (`yecies2011_cmet`). Conversely, intracellular sn-1,2-diacylglycerol (DAG) accumulation activates novel $\text{PKC}\varepsilon$, which phosphorylates the human insulin receptor at Thr1160 in the catalytic activation loop to inhibit `InsR` tyrosine kinase activity (`nasce2022_antiox`, `cruzvillarreal2026_kp`), while $\text{PKC}\zeta$ also mediates negative feedback on IRS-1 by phosphorylating Ser323/Ser318 (and Ser498/Ser570) (`cruzvillarreal2026_kp`), and hepatic $\text{PKC}\delta$ promotes lipogenesis via SREBP-1c, FAS, and ACC upregulation (`cruzvillarreal2026_kp`).
- Oxidative stress, NOX3, and mitochondrial ROS in JNK-mediated hepatic insulin resistance: Saturated fatty acids (palmitate, but not monounsaturated oleate) and pro-inflammatory $\text{TNF-}\alpha$ induce hepatocyte reactive oxygen species (ROS) that activate JNK and p38MAPK, inhibiting IRS-1/IRS-2 tyrosine phosphorylation, Akt and GSK phosphorylation, and glycogen synthesis while increasing gluconeogenesis (`nakamura2009_jbc`, `li2010_febslett`, `gao2010_jbc`, `nasce2022_antiox`). In HepG2 cells and *db/db* mouse livers, `Li` and `Gao` et al. demonstrated that $\text{TNF-}\alpha$ and palmitate upregulate NADPH oxidase 3 (`NOX3`), and that siRNA knockdown of `NOX3` prevents ROS generation, JNK/p38MAPK activation, and glycogen depletion (`li2010_febslett`, `gao2010_jbc`; single-lab primary series by Jian Li's group, cited across metabolic studies). In rat H4IIEC3 hepatocytes, `Nakamura` et al. showed that palmitate-induced JNK activation and IRS-2/Akt inhibition are also driven by mitochondrial respiratory chain ROS resulting from accelerated CPT-1-dependent fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation (`nakamura2009_jbc`).
- AMPK and $G_{q/11}$/PLC signaling in hepatic glucose and lipid control: AMPK directly interacts with and phosphorylates SREBP-1c at Ser372 (and SREBP-2) in response to metformin and polyphenols (such as S17834), which blocks SREBP-1c proteolytic cleavage and nuclear translocation and attenuates hepatic steatosis and hyperlipidemia in insulin-resistant mice (`li2011_cmet`). Conversely, activation of hepatocyte $G\alpha_{q/11}$-coupled GPCRs stimulates phospholipase $\text{C}\beta$ ($\text{PLC}\beta$) to hydrolyze $\text{PIP}_2$ into $\text{IP}_3$ (triggering ER $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ release) and DAG (`kimura2020_biomol`, `cruzvillarreal2026_kp`). In transgenic `Hep-Rq` (`Hep-GqD`) mice expressing a clozapine-N-oxide (CNO)-sensitive $G_q$-coupled designer receptor selectively in hepatocytes (single-lab chemogenetic model by Jürgen Wess's group, reviewed in `kimura2020_biomol`), acute $G_q$ activation stimulates glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis (upregulating `Pck1`), and lipogenic genes (`Acyl`, `Acc`, `Fas`), raising blood glucose to the same magnitude as maximally effective glucagon (`li2013_endocrinology`, `kimura2020_biomol`). Furthermore, hepatic expression of the endogenous $G_q$-coupled vasopressin $\text{V}_{1\text{b}}$ receptor is markedly elevated in *ob/ob* mice, and selective $\text{V}_{1\text{b}}$ antagonism reduces pyruvate-stimulated glucose excursions in vivo (`li2013_endocrinology`).
- Sex differences and estrogen ($\text{E}_2$ / $\text{ER}\alpha$) regulation of UPR and hepatic insulin signaling: Across multiple rodent models, sex and $17\beta$-estradiol ($\text{E}_2$) strongly modulate both pancreatic islet UPR and hepatic insulin sensitivity (`zhu2014_ajpendo`, `depaoli2024_ijms`, `alves2025_pathophys`, `depaoli2025_jes`, `franczak2026_biorxiv`, `monteillet2026_biorxiv`). In the sexually dimorphic polygenic TALLYHO/JngJ mouse model of T2D (`depaoli2025_jes`) and the $\text{ApoE}^{-/-}:\text{Ins2}^{+/\text{Akita}}$ model (`depaoli2024_ijms`), males develop chronic hyperglycemia (5-week fasting glucose $27.55\text{ mmol/L}$ in TALLYHO males vs. $7.9\text{ mmol/L}$ in intact females) while intact females remain normoglycemic; ovariectomy (OVX) causes females to develop chronic hyperglycemia ($>15\text{ mmol/L}$ by 12 weeks) accompanied by elevated islet GRP78/GRP94 and pro-apoptotic GADD153/CHOP and reduced $\beta$-cell mass (`depaoli2025_jes`), whereas $\text{E}_2$ supplementation represses apoptotic UPR and enhances adaptive UPR (`depaoli2024_ijms`). In the liver, hepatocyte estrogen receptor-$\alpha$ ($\text{ER}\alpha$) is required in male mice on an HFD to maintain insulin-stimulated Akt-Ser473 phosphorylation, nuclear FOXO1 exclusion, ACC phosphorylation, and suppression of endogenous glucose production during hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamps (`zhu2014_ajpendo`). Conversely, AAV-mediated hepatic $\text{ER}\alpha$ overexpression in male C57BL/6J mice reduces body fat by 28%, increases hepatocyte basal (19%) and maximal (34%) oxygen consumption, lowers HFD-induced hepatic triglycerides by 43%, and improves hepatic insulin sensitivity by 145% (`alves2025_pathophys`).

Contested
- Gain vs. partial loss of GRP78 function in metabolic disease: While adenoviral overexpression of GRP78 specifically in the liver of *ob/ob* mice inhibits SREBP-1c cleavage and improves hepatic steatosis and insulin sensitivity (`kammoun2009_jci`), whole-body `Grp78(+/-)` heterozygosity in male HFD-fed mice also protects against diet-induced obesity, hepatic steatosis, and insulin resistance by triggering a compensatory adaptive UPR (upregulating ATF6, EDEM, and other ER chaperones in white adipose tissue) (`ye2010_diabetes`), and systemic infusion of extracellular GRP78 protein drives insulin resistance and NASH (`angelini2022_natcommun`). Thus, intracellular ER-luminal chaperone sufficiency is protective, whereas extracellular/circulating GRP78 is pathogenic and partial genetic haploinsufficiency induces hormetic UPR compensation (`kammoun2009_jci`, `ye2010_diabetes`, `angelini2022_natcommun`).
- Relative roles of Akt/mTORC1 vs. atypical $\text{PKC}\lambda/\zeta$ and FoxO1 in hepatic lipogenesis: `Matsumoto` et al. (`matsumoto2003_jci`) and `Taniguchi` et al. (`taniguchi2006_cmet`) established that PI3K-dependent activation of hepatic `Srebp1c` expression is mediated by $\text{PKC}\lambda/\zeta$ rather than Akt (noting in `matsumoto2003_jci` that dominant-negative Akt did not prevent insulin-induced `Srebp1c`). In contrast, `Yecies` et al. (`yecies2011_cmet`) demonstrated that Akt is required to stimulate hepatic SREBP-1c and de novo lipogenesis via parallel mTORC1 activation and `Insig2a` suppression, and `Yang` et al. (`yang2026_cells`) noted that hepatocyte FoxO1 actually suppresses lipogenesis by inhibiting SREBP-1c and glucokinase while promoting ATGL-mediated lipolysis (such that hepatocyte FoxO1/3/4 triple deletion worsens diet-induced steatosis and fibrosis).
- Requirement of hepatocyte $\text{ER}\alpha$ for estrogen-mediated resolution of post-ovariectomy hepatic steatosis: Earlier developmental liver-specific $\text{ER}\alpha$ knockout studies cited in `zhu2014_ajpendo` and `franczak2026_biorxiv` indicated that hepatocyte $\text{ER}\alpha$ is required for $\text{E}_2$ treatment to reduce liver triglycerides after OVX. However, a 2026 bioRxiv preprint using inducible AAV-Cre hepatocyte $\text{ER}\alpha$ deletion (`LERKO`) in middle-aged (36–40-week-old) ovariectomized mice found that $\text{E}_2$ replacement substantially reduced hepatic triglycerides, de novo lipogenesis proteins, and mitochondrial $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ emission in both control and `LERKO` mice, although `LERKO` mice retained higher absolute hepatic triglycerides and completely failed to increase palmitoyl-CoA-supported mitochondrial respiration in response to $\text{E}_2$ (`franczak2026_biorxiv`).
- Expression and physiological role of NOX3 in primary mammalian hepatocytes vs. HepG2 cells: While `li2010_febslett` and `gao2010_jbc` identified `NOX3` as the predominant source of $\text{TNF-}\alpha$- and palmitate-induced ROS in human HepG2 hepatoma cells and *db/db* mouse livers, `nasce2022_antiox` notes that while NOX1, NOX2, NOX4, DUOX1, and DUOX2 are established in primary hepatocytes, `NOX3` expression in primary hepatocytes outside the HepG2 cell line remains undetermined, and `nakamura2009_jbc` attributed palmitate-induced ROS and JNK activation in rat H4IIEC3 hepatocytes to mitochondrial $\beta$-oxidation electron transport chain flux.
- Sex-specific $\beta$-cell PERK/UPR adaptation upon NCK1 loss (single-lab 2026 preprint): In `monteillet2026_biorxiv`, $\beta$-cell-specific deletion of the UPR/tyrosine kinase adaptor `NCK1` (`NCK1betaKO`) impaired basal PERK signaling and glucose-stimulated insulin secretion ex vivo in both sexes (contrasting with prior MIN6 cell studies), but under a 23-week high-fat/high-sucrose diet caused opposite PERK responses in vivo—enhancing PERK signaling, $\beta$-cell proliferation, and islet size in males while suppressing PERK signaling and proliferative genes (`CyclinA`/`CyclinB` reduced by ~50%) and increasing small immature islets in females (`monteillet2026_biorxiv`).

Unknown
- Atomic-level 3D conformational dynamics of post-translationally modified GRP78: High-resolution cryo-EM, X-ray, or HDX-MS structures of full-length GRP78 carrying site-specific modifications (such as Thr453/473/500 phosphorylation, Arg470/492 ADP-ribosylation, or competing Cys420 S-palmitoylation vs. S-sulfhydration) have not yet been resolved (`li2026_theranostics`).
- Identity of the endogenous hepatocyte cell-surface partner coupling extracellular GRP78 or $G_{q/11}$ ligands to metabolic insulin resistance: While cell-surface GRP78 is known to associate with GPI-anchored proteins (such as CD109) and PI3K p85 in tumor cells (`zhang2013_plosone`, `li2026_theranostics`) and circulating jejunal GRP78 induces hepatic insulin resistance and NASH in rats (`angelini2022_natcommun`), the exact hepatocyte surface receptor complex mediating circulating GRP78 uptake or signaling in MASLD/T2D remains undefined (`angelini2022_natcommun`, `li2026_theranostics`).
- Sex-by-treatment interactions for GRP78 inhibitors or $G_{q/11}$ antagonists in hepatic insulin resistance: None of the primary studies on GRP78 genetic/pharmacological modulation (`kammoun2009_jci`, `ye2010_diabetes`, `samanta2021_canres`, `angelini2022_natcommun`), NOX3/JNK (`li2010_febslett`, `gao2010_jbc`), or hepatocyte $G_q$ signaling (`li2013_endocrinology`) compared male and female cohorts side-by-side in a factorial design to test whether sex or estrogen status modifies pathway sensitivity.

D. CONTRADICTIONS

- Hepatic GRP78 overexpression (`kammoun2009_jci`) vs. `Grp78(+/-)` heterozygosity (`ye2010_diabetes`) and recombinant GRP78 infusion (`angelini2022_natcommun`): `kammoun2009_jci` showed that increasing hepatic GRP78 expression in *ob/ob* mice inhibits SREBP-1c activation and reverses steatosis and insulin resistance, whereas `ye2010_diabetes` showed that reducing GRP78 gene dosage by 50% (`Grp78+/-`) in HFD-fed mice also protects against steatosis and insulin resistance via adaptive UPR hormesis in adipose tissue, and `angelini2022_natcommun` showed that infusing circulating GRP78 protein provokes insulin resistance and NASH while anti-GRP78 monoclonal antibodies reverse it.
- PI3K–$\text{PKC}\lambda$ vs. PI3K–Akt control of hepatic SREBP-1c (`matsumoto2003_jci` vs. `yecies2011_cmet`): `matsumoto2003_jci` reported that dominant-negative Akt failed to inhibit (and instead augmented) insulin-induced `Srebp1c` expression in primary hepatocytes while $\text{PKC}\lambda$ deletion reduced refeeding-induced `Srebp1c` by ~50%, whereas `yecies2011_cmet` demonstrated in `LTsc1KO` mice that attenuation of hepatic Akt signaling abolishes SREBP-1c activation and de novo lipogenesis even when mTORC1 is constitutively active.
- Hepatocyte $\text{ER}\alpha$ requirement for $\text{E}_2$-mediated triglyceride reduction after ovariectomy (`zhu2014_ajpendo` vs. `franczak2026_biorxiv`): While earlier developmental liver-specific $\text{ER}\alpha$ knockout models cited in `zhu2014_ajpendo` and `franczak2026_biorxiv` indicated that hepatocyte $\text{ER}\alpha$ is required for estrogen treatment to correct post-OVX fatty liver, `franczak2026_biorxiv` found that in adult-onset AAV-Cre `LERKO` ovariectomized middle-aged mice, $\text{E}_2$ replacement significantly lowered hepatic triglycerides and de novo lipogenesis proteins independently of hepatocyte $\text{ER}\alpha$ (while requiring hepatocyte $\text{ER}\alpha$ strictly for $\text{E}_2$-stimulated mitochondrial respiration).
- Effect of NCK1 loss on $\beta$-cell PERK signaling and proinsulin synthesis (`monteillet2026_biorxiv` vs. prior in vitro studies): `monteillet2026_biorxiv` found that acute or stable `Nck1` knockdown in INS-1 cells and $\beta$-cell-specific `Nck1` deletion in mouse islets reduced basal PERK/eIF2$\alpha$ phosphorylation, `Atf4`/`Chop` expression, and proinsulin/insulin content, directly contradicting earlier reports in MIN6 cells (discussed in `monteillet2026_biorxiv`) where `Nck1` silencing enhanced adaptive PERK activity and proinsulin synthesis.

E. OPEN QUESTIONS

- [Inference] Given that `YUM70` inhibits GRP78 ATPase activity allosterically by binding to the C-terminal substrate-binding domain ($\text{SBD}\beta$ residues Ser452, Gln458, Thr462, Lys464) and triggers PERK/IRE1$\alpha$ dissociation and ER stress (`samanta2021_canres`), how do peptide inhibitors that bind GRP78 avoid triggering pro-apoptotic CHOP activation in hepatocytes and $\beta$-cells while suppressing GRP78 ATPase activity?
- [Inference] Since hepatocyte $\text{ER}\alpha$ signaling is essential for insulin-stimulated Akt-Ser473 phosphorylation, nuclear FoxO1 exclusion, and ACC phosphorylation in obese male mice (`zhu2014_ajpendo`, `alves2025_pathophys`) and for $\text{E}_2$-stimulated mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation in ovariectomized female mice (`franczak2026_biorxiv`), does estrogen depletion in VCD/OVX models sensitize the liver to $G_{q/11}$–PKC–JNK-driven insulin resistance, and would intact Cycling females respond differently from males or OVX females to GRP78/AMPK modulators?
- [Inference] Because `Cys420` of GRP78 is a competitive switch between ZDHHC9-mediated S-palmitoylation (which stabilizes ER-retained GRP78 and suppresses UPR sensors) and $\text{H}_2\text{S}$-mediated S-sulfhydration (which dissociates IRE1$\alpha$ and polarizes macrophages toward M1) (`li2026_theranostics`), does metabolic oxidative stress or NOX3/mitochondrial ROS (`nakamura2009_jbc`, `gao2010_jbc`) alter GRP78 post-translational modifications in hepatocytes and adipose macrophages?
